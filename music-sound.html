Music & Sound Workbook

The words for what music sounds like: genres and folk traditions, instruments, rhythm and tempo, harmony, song structure, voices, production and sound design. Every card shows the thing it names, most of them play a short sketch synthesized in your browser, and each one gives you a phrase and a style tag to paste into a prompt.

One melody, every room

Production words are easiest to hear when the notes stay the same. Pick a tune, then play it through each treatment. The melody never changes, only the room, the tape and the gear it passes through. Tap a name to open its card.

Anatomy of a music prompt

A music prompt that lands names a genre and an era, two or three instruments with a job each, a feel and a tempo, a voice, and how it was recorded. Here is what that looks like for three different jobs. Tap any word to jump to its card.

  • Describe, don't nameMost music generators refuse artist and song names. Say what the artist sounds like instead: "reverb-drenched baritone, pedal steel, slow waltz". Every card here gives you that description.
  • Genre plus era plus place"Country" is a thousand things. "1970s Texas outlaw country" is one. Stack two genre words at most, then pin the decade.
  • Give each instrument a job"Fingerpicked guitar carries the verse, pedal steel answers the vocal" beats a list of five instruments that all fight for the middle.
  • Say what's missing"No drums", "no autotune" or "no choir" removes the generator's default habits. Use the Leave out box in the tray.
  • Tempo is a number"Slow" means 60 to one model and 90 to another. Write the BPM, and the time signature if it isn't 4/4.

Sound by mood

Start from a feeling or a scene and read across: a genre, the instruments that carry it, a groove, a harmonic color, a voice, and a production treatment. Every name links to its card.
