The camera words that make an image prompt look like a real photograph: shot sizes, angles, lenses, lighting setups, times of day, film stocks and the photographers people point at. Each card has a drawn example, how to recognize it, and a phrase you can paste into a prompt.

Anatomy of a photo prompt

Photographers describe a picture in roughly this order. You rarely need every slot, but each one you fill removes a guess the image model would otherwise make. Tap any word to jump to its card.

Shot size+Angle+Subject+Lens & focus+Light+Time & weather+Film or grade+Genre or reference
  • Camera words beat adjectives"85mm at f/1.8" says more than "beautiful blurry background", and image models were trained on captions full of exactly those numbers.
  • Tell one light storyPick one main light and say where it comes from. "Golden hour" plus "neon" plus "studio softbox" fights itself and comes out flat.
  • Name the film to get the color"Kodak Portra 400" carries a whole palette, grain and skin tone in three words.
  • Describe the look, not just the nameSome image tools ignore or refuse living artists' names. Pair a name with what it means: "in the spirit of Saul Leiter: muted color, shot through a rainy window".
