Materials & Textures

The names for what things are made of and how they were finished: woods, stones, cloth, metals, glass, sheens, wear, and the print processes and papers that give a poster or a book its feel. Each card has a drawn swatch, how to recognize it, and a phrase you can paste into a prompt.

Anatomy of a material prompt

A surface is described in layers: what it is, how it was finished, what time and hands have done to it, and the light that shows it off. For printed things, add the process and the paper. Tap any word to jump to its card.

Object+Material+Finish+Wear+Light that shows it+Print process+Paper
  • Name the species, not the category"Rift-sawn white oak" and "Calacatta marble" each carry a color, a grain and a price point. "Wood" and "marble" leave all three to chance.
  • Say the sheen separatelyThe same walnut can be matte oiled or mirror lacquered. Image models guess gloss from the material unless you tell them.
  • Raking light sells textureA low light skimming across the surface is what makes linen, plaster and letterpress read as real. Ask for it by name.
  • Imperfection is the realismMisregistration on a riso print or edge wear on a brass handle tells the eye the thing was made, not rendered. Ask for a little, in a specific place.

Same feeling, every surface

A mood comes from several surface choices pushing the same way. Find the feeling on the left and read across for a material, finish, wear and print that agree with each other.
