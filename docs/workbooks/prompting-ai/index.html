Anatomy of a prompt that works

Most prompts that hold up in production have the same parts: a role, the goal and the reader, the inputs fenced off in tags, an example or two, room to think, a defined output, and a way to check the result. Here is how that reads for three different jobs. Tap any phrase to jump to its card.

  • Explain it like a new hireWrite the prompt you would give a smart colleague on their first day: what the task is, who it's for, why it matters, and what good looks like. Missing context causes more bad output than missing tricks.
  • Show, then tellTwo or three well-chosen examples steer format and judgment harder than a paragraph of adjectives. Vary them so the model copies the pattern, not the content.
  • Make room to think, then checkOn anything with steps, ask for reasoning before the answer, and keep it in its own tags so you can strip it. Then grade outputs against criteria written down in advance.
  • Change one thing at a timeKeep a small test set and rerun it after every edit. A prompt that only gets read, never tested, drifts the moment the inputs change.
