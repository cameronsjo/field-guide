Anatomy of a brand prompt

A logo or identity prompt that lands names the kind of mark, a style, a personality to hit, a typeface direction and the places it has to work. Here is what that sounds like for three different jobs. Tap any word to jump to its card.

  • Say the kind of logo first"A lettermark" or "a combination mark with a pictorial symbol" removes more guesswork than any adjective about the brand.
  • Give the mark a job and a sizeSay where it has to live: a 16px favicon, a van door, an embroidered cap. Small and rough uses force simpler, better marks.
  • Pick an archetype, then say what it isn't"Explorer, not outdoorsy cliché" or "Sage, not stuffy" gives a direction and a fence.
  • Ask for a system, not just a logoA color, a typeface, one graphic device and a few applications is what makes a mark feel like a brand.

Identity by kind of business

Start from what the business is and read across: a kind of logo, a mark style, an archetype, a type direction and a system piece that usually suits it. Every name links to its card. These are starting points; breaking them on purpose is how brands stand out.
