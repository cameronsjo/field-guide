Film & Cinematography

The words filmmakers use for how a movie is shot, staged and cut: coverage and the 180-degree rule, blocking, signature shots, edits and montage, film lighting and grades, aspect ratios and formats, genres, movements, and the directors and cinematographers people point at. Each card has a drawn frame, storyboard or floor plan, how to recognize it, and a phrase you can paste into a prompt.

Anatomy of a film prompt

A shot list or a call sheet describes each shot in roughly this order. A still needs the first half; a video clip or a whole scene needs the rest. Tap any word to jump to its card.

Format & aspect+Shot+Staging+Who does what+Light+Color+Edit+Genre or name
  • Say "film still", then prove it"Cinematic" alone means very little. A ratio, a format and a light source ("2.39:1, 35mm, lit by one practical lamp") make a frame look like it came out of a movie.
  • One shot per promptImage and video models make one continuous shot. Edits and montage belong in a shot list: ask Claude to break the scene into numbered shots, then render each one.
  • Motivate the lightName where the light comes from inside the scene (a window, a fridge, a passing car). It reads as real, and it keeps the image from looking evenly lit by nothing.
  • Pair a name with what it meansSome tools ignore or refuse directors' names. Write "in the spirit of Wong Kar-wai: step-printed smear, green fluorescent light, a narrow corridor" so the look survives either way.
